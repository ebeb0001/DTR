\chapter*{Abstract}
This thesis investigates the construction of a simulation-oriented railway digital twin using SUMO and heterogeneous 
railway infrastructure and operational data. The objective is to transform real-world datasets into simulation-ready 
artefacts and to evaluate how different levels of network representation affect simulation feasibility.

A data-driven pipeline is developed to generate SUMO-compatible networks, reconstruct train trips, execute simulations, 
and analyse outputs. Two modelling levels are compared: a macro-level station-to-station graph and a micro-level model 
representing tracks, platforms, switches, segmented sections, and routing structures.

The results show that the macro-level model reaches 100\% station and track coverage, while the micro-level model reaches 
99.82\% station coverage and 99.42\% platform coverage. The micro-level reconstruction also detects 12,343 switches, 
compared with 10,379 in the reference data. The generation time increases from approximately 331 seconds at the macro 
level to 775 seconds at the micro level, reflecting the higher complexity of detailed infrastructure modelling.

The macro-level model provides a robust basis for integrating real punctuality data into SUMO simulations. The 
micro-level model supports random-trip simulations but remains limited when integrating real operational data, mainly 
because valid paths cannot always be reconstructed through the dual graph.

Overall, this work shows that infrastructure coverage alone is not sufficient to validate a railway digital twin. The 
generated network must also be topologically consistent and operationally routable. The thesis provides a first 
foundation for SUMO-based railway digital twin development and identifies the main challenges for future improvements.
